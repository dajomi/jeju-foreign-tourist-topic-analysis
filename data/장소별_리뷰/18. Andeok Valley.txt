Hidden evergreen valley
While this is  just of the roadway, it’s fairly free of visitors.   The path goes alongside running water (or not - depending on rainfall).    There was about a 30m section where the path ended and we had to walk from rock to rock before the path restarted.    On the other side was a cave - Rock Shade Residence - which olden people used as shelter.   Entire area is a beautiful evergreen valley, well worth a visit.
Magic of a Hidden Valley
Oh gosh!  This is definitely one of the must-sees of Jeju Island!A short 15min (mostly flat) loop, and you will be rewarded with babbling brooks and crystal clear waters overlooked by towering cliffs.  Best of all, this is off the beaten path, and there were virtually no tourists around.  Oh...the tranquility.Some parts of the walk involve jumping from rock to rock in the streams.  Not a problem generally (my kids found it fun), but if you have mobility issues, you may want to bear this in mind.We would like to thank Victor of Jejueco Suites for helping us discover this hidden jem.
Enchanting Evergreen Valley
A quiet valley in an evergreen forest, the place is almost free of foreign tourists and allows one to capture the most tranquil moments of nature. Where cliffs and reflective pools of crystal clear water meet, lined by stones, guarded by trees and filled with crisp air. The sunlight peeking through the trees permeates a serene and mesmerising ambience in the valley. Look out for the stone wells and Rock Shade Residence along the way. The Rock Shade Residence formed from volcanic lava were used by ancient inhabitants of Jeju Island as shelter. It is said that Andeok Valley has water flowing all seasons long.  With such charming beauty, its no wonder Chusa Kim Jeong-hee, a renowned Joseon-era scholar, used to visit here for contemplation and perhaps, inspiration. Spring and fall would be great for light trekking while the cool forest provides the ultimate summer shade. Nearby attractions: O'Sulloc Tea Museum, Camellia Hill and Jungmun Resorts.Trivia: Andeok Valley was also the filming location of historical K-drama "Slave Hunter" (Chuno in Korean)
